# WARN notices may be published

Made Nov 23, 2026, in chat. California's EDD, Washington's ESD and the Texas Workforce Commission all publish their WARN layoff notices for the public, but none of them states reuse terms (checked Nov 21; see `registry/sources.yaml`).

| Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| The three WARN sources are cleared with conditions and may be published | The notices are filings employers are legally required to make, published by the state for the public. Facts in them aren't copyrightable, and the lab publishes facts, not the agencies' files |
| Only facts are published: employer, headcount, notice and effective dates, and place. The agency is credited; workers' names and contact details are never used | Keeps the use to what the notices exist to disclose. Washington made employee information in notices confidential |
| The existing rules stay: a notice is never called an AI layoff, the employer before any parentheses is the one counted, coverage limits are stated, and WARN totals are never added to restructuring charges | They're what make WARN figures honest, whatever the license |
| Each agency is still asked in writing; a reply that sets terms replaces this ruling for that state | The ruling is a good-faith reading, not legal advice, and the agencies' own word is better |
| Texas is read from the state open data portal first, with the City of Austin's copy of the same dataset as a fallback | Ingest from the original publisher where possible |

The same approach was used for congressional financial disclosures (confirmed Nov 10).
